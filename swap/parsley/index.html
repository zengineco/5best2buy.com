What to use instead of parsley

It depends entirely on what it's doing in the dish. Here's the job, what else does it, and what to skip.

Advertisement

Fresh green finish that cuts fat

Flat-leaf parsley is mostly water plus volatile green aroma. Scattered on at the end it lifts the top of a rich dish without adding a flavour of its own that competes with the sauce.

Pale inner celery leavesRan out
1:1 chopped
More bitter and saltier-tasting, and stronger, so a little goes further. Good on braises and soups, fights with delicate fish.

What doesn't work: Dried parsley. What parsley gives at the end is volatile aroma and wet crunch, and drying removes both; on steak frites it is green dust that tastes faintly of hay.

Bulk raw green in a chopped herb sauce (chimichurri, gremolata, salsa verde)

Here parsley is the body of the sauce rather than a garnish, so the swap has to survive being chopped, salted and left in oil and acid for an hour without collapsing into slime.

Cilantro, stems includedRan out
1:1 by volume
Sharper and more perfumed. A chimichurri made with it tastes Colombian rather than Argentine.
Where to buy it →
Half parsley, half mintRan out
replace up to half the parsley by volume
Cooler and sweeter, and the sauce reads Levantine. Very good on lamb, odd on steak.
Where to buy it →
Carrot tops plus extra lemonRan out
1:1, plus 1 extra tsp lemon juice per cup of herb
Slightly bitter, which the extra acid balances. The sauce comes out darker green.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: Basil in a chimichurri. It bruises black within minutes in oil and vinegar and its sweetness fights the garlic and oregano; what you get is a broken pesto that tastes of neither dish.

Parsley stems carry more flavour than the leaves and none of the bitterness. Chop them into the sauce and keep the woody ends for stock.
