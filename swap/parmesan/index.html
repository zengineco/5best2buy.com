What to use instead of parmesan

It depends entirely on what it's doing in the dish. Here's the job, what else does it, and what to skip.

Advertisement

Salty savoury seasoning grated over a finished dish

Two years of ageing breaks much of the protein down into free glutamate, so it behaves like a seasoning that happens to be cheese.

Grana PadanoRan out
1:1
Milder and less crystalline because it is aged for less time. Cheaper, and it melts a little more readily.
Where to buy it →
Pecorino RomanoRan out
3/4 the amount
Much saltier and sharper with a clear sheep note. Cut the salt in the dish or it will be inedible.
Where to buy it →
Aged gouda or a long-aged cheddarRan out
1:1
Sweeter and fattier, and it melts into puddles instead of staying as a dusting. Good on soup, wrong on pasta.
Where to buy it →
Nutritional yeastPlant-based
3 tbsp + 1/4 tsp salt per 1/4 cup grated parmesan
Savoury and cheesy-smelling with no fat and no melt at all. It sits on the surface as a powder and dulls if it goes into hot liquid.
What doesn't work: Pre-grated shaker parmesan. It is coated with cellulose or starch to stop it caking, so it refuses to melt, sits on the pasta as sandy grit and thickens a sauce into paste.

Emulsifier and body in a pasta sauce

Its protein and fat bind with the starch in the pasta water into a smooth coating. Too much heat and the protein tightens and the fat splits out.

Pecorino RomanoRan out
3/4 the amount
Emulsifies the same and tastes far sharper. It breaks more readily, so keep the pan off the heat while you toss.
Where to buy it →
Grana PadanoRan out
1:1
Slightly softer and milder, and it comes together with less effort.
Where to buy it →
Cashew cream with nutritional yeastPlant-based
1/4 cup cashew cream + 2 tbsp nutritional yeast per 1/2 cup grated parmesan
Coats the pasta convincingly and thickens as it stands, so loosen it with pasta water. Sweeter, and it will not string or crisp.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: Mozzarella or another young melting cheese. Too much moisture and stretchy casein, so it strings into a lump around the tongs and leaves the sauce thin and greasy underneath.

Keep the rinds. Dropped into a soup or a sauce they give you the savoury depth without any grating.
