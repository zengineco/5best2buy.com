What to use instead of bread

It depends entirely on what it's doing in the dish. Here's the job, what else does it, and what to skip.

Advertisement

Starch that soaks up custard and holds its shape

Stale bread has dried cell walls that pull in liquid and stay intact, which is why a bread pudding has distinct pieces instead of being a single wet mass.

Brioche or challah, dried out firstRan out
1:1 by weight, dried in a 300F oven for 10 min, and halve the soaking time
Richer and sweeter, browns much faster, and it drinks custard in about half the time.
Where to buy it →
Day-old croissantsRan out
1:1 by weight
Buttery and layered, with a crisp shattery top. The pudding is greasier and needs less butter in the dish.
Where to buy it →
Leftover cinnamon rolls or panettoneRan out
1:1 by weight, and cut the sugar in the custard by a third
Much sweeter with spice and dried fruit already built in. Without the sugar cut it is sickly.
Where to buy it →
Gluten-free sandwich loaf, dried hardGluten-free
1:1 by weight, soaked 30 min instead of 15
Takes liquid slowly then turns to paste if you push it. The set is denser and wetter throughout.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: Fresh soft supermarket white. There is no dry structure left in it, so it dissolves into the custard and the pudding bakes as a uniform wet paste with no pieces.

Panade that keeps meatballs and meatloaf tender

Bread soaked in milk traps water in a starch gel that stays put while the meat proteins tighten, which is why the meatball does not squeeze itself dry.

Dried breadcrumbs + milkRan out
1/2 cup crumbs + 1/2 cup milk per 2 slices of bread
Tighter and slightly denser meatball. Let it sit 5 min so the crumbs actually hydrate.
Where to buy it →
Panko + milkRan out
1 cup panko + 1/2 cup milk per 2 slices
Lighter and springier. Panko drinks more liquid than it looks like it will, so let it stand.
Where to buy it →
Rolled oats or cooked riceGluten-free
1/3 cup rolled oats or 1/2 cup cooked rice per 2 slices
Oats read faintly sweet and go gluey if you overmix. Rice stays as visible grains in the slice.
Where to buy it →
Crushed crackers + milkRan out
3/4 cup crushed crackers + 1/2 cup milk per 2 slices, and cut the salt in the mix
Browns darker and tastes saltier. Skipping the salt adjustment makes the loaf inedible.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: Flour stirred straight into the mince. It binds, but with no soaked crumb holding water the meat contracts and squeezes itself dry, giving a firm bouncy loaf.

Body in a blended cold soup or dip

Soaked crumb blends into an emulsion that carries olive oil, which is how salmorejo gets creamy without a drop of cream.

Stale sourdough or baguette, crusts removedRan out
1:1 by weight
Sourdough brings a tang that shows up clearly in a tomato soup. Take the crusts off or the colour goes grey.
Where to buy it →
Dried breadcrumbsRan out
1/2 cup crumbs per 2 oz of bread, soaked 10 min
Blends smoother and faster with noticeably less body. Add a little more to compensate.
Where to buy it →
Ground walnutsGluten-free
1/2 cup ground walnuts per 1/2 cup crumbs
Much richer and oilier, and the dip goes darker. This is the muhammara direction anyway.
Where to buy it →
Cooked white riceGluten-free
1/2 cup cooked rice per 2 oz bread
Blends thick and glossy. Run the blender too long and it turns gummy and will not come back.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: A cornstarch slurry to thicken a cold soup. Raw it tastes chalky and cooked it sets to a gel, and neither one gives the emulsified bready body the dish is built on.
