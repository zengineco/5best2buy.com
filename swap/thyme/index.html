What to use instead of fresh thyme

It depends entirely on what it's doing in the dish. Here's the job, what else does it, and what to skip.

Advertisement

Woody herb that infuses through a long braise or roast

Thymol is stable and fat-soluble, so unlike basil or parsley thyme survives hours in a pot. The whole sprig goes in early and comes out at the end having given everything to the liquid.

Dried thymeRan out
1 tsp dried per 1 tbsp fresh leaves, or per 4 sprigs
More concentrated and slightly dusty, and it disperses through the sauce instead of staying on a stem you can fish out later.
Where to buy it →
Fresh marjoram or oregano sprigsRan out
1:1 by sprig
Sweeter and more peppery. Close enough in a coq au vin, noticeably wrong in a jerk marinade.
Where to buy it →
Winter savoryRan out
1:1 by sprig
Sharper and more peppery, and closer to thyme than anything else on the shelf. Traditional with beans for good reason.
Where to buy it →
Herbes de ProvenceRan out
1 tsp per 4 sprigs fresh thyme
Thyme is the base of it, with savory and rosemary alongside and often lavender. The lavender is what gives it away.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: Rosemary at the same measure. It is several times more pungent and its pine character does not fade with cooking; a sprig in a coq au vin steamrolls the wine and the mushrooms, and in a Jamaican pot it argues with the allspice outright.

Fresh green finish on mushrooms, eggs or a tart

Picked off the stem and added late, thyme keeps its lemony top note, which is what lifts something rich and beige like a mushroom tart or a plate of scrambled eggs.

Lemon thymeRan out
1:1
The same herb with the citrus turned up. On mushrooms on toast it is arguably the better choice.
Where to buy it →
Fresh marjoram leavesRan out
1:1
Sweeter and softer with no lemon at all. It bruises easily, so scatter it whole rather than chopping.
Where to buy it →
Fresh oregano, chopped fineRan out
use 2/3 as much
More assertive and peppery, and it pushes a mushroom tart toward tasting like pizza.
Where to buy it →
Chopped chives with a scrape of lemon zestRan out
1 tbsp chives + 1/4 tsp zest per 1 tbsp thyme leaves
Oniony rather than woody, with the zest covering the missing citrus note. Good on eggs, poor on lamb.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: Dried thyme sprinkled on at the end. It never rehydrates on a dry surface, so it stays as gritty specks that taste of the jar; dried thyme has to go in early enough to soften into some liquid.
