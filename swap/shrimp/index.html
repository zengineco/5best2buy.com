What to use instead of shrimp

It depends entirely on what it's doing in the dish. Here's the job, what else does it, and what to skip.

Advertisement

Fast-cooking sweet shellfish in a hot pan or broth

Shrimp muscle is short-fibred with almost no connective tissue, so it goes from raw to done in about two minutes; the sweetness peaks the moment the flesh turns opaque and fades a minute later.

Bay or small sea scallopsRan out
1:1 by weight, same 2 minutes in the pan
Sweeter and softer. No snap, so a stir fry loses its bite and the pieces want to fall apart when you toss them.
Where to buy it →
Lump crab meatRan out
1:1 by weight, folded in off the heat
Shreds instead of holding a curl, so it disappears into rice or pasta rather than sitting on top of it.
Where to buy it →
Firm white fish (monkfish, halibut, cod loin) in 1-inch cubesRan out
1:1 by weight, 3 to 4 minutes, turned once
Flakes apart if you stir it, so add it whole and leave it alone. Milder and it soaks up sauce instead of resisting it.
Where to buy it →
King oyster mushroom, cut in 1-inch rounds and cross-hatchedPlant-based
8 oz mushroom per 8 oz shrimp, plus 1/4 tsp fine salt
Genuinely chewy and it takes a hard sear, but there is no brine at all behind it, so the dish tastes flat until you salt it up.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: Pre-cooked frozen cocktail shrimp in anything that gets cooked again. The protein is already fully set, so a second cooking wrings water out of the muscle and leaves a rubbery ring with no sweetness left in it.

Concentrated briny savouriness (dried shrimp, shrimp in a stock or paste)

Dried shrimp has been salted and dehydrated until its glutamates are concentrated. It is a seasoning that happens to be an animal, doing the same work anchovy does in an Italian sauce.

Oil-packed anchovy fillet, mashed into the hot oilRan out
1 fillet per 1 tbsp dried shrimp
Fishier and saltier up front, less sweet. It melts completely, so you lose the little chewy specks.
Where to buy it →
Fish sauce, added with the liquidRan out
2 tsp per 1 tbsp dried shrimp
All of the savour, none of the texture, and it carries salt with it, so pull back elsewhere.
Where to buy it →
Shrimp paste (belacan, bagoong), toasted firstRan out
1/2 tsp toasted paste per 1 tbsp dried shrimp
Far more pungent, and untoasted it tastes raw and soapy. Toast it in a dry pan until it smells nutty.
Where to buy it →
Dried shiitake, soaked and minced, plus saltPlant-based
3 tbsp minced soaked shiitake + 1/4 tsp salt per 1 tbsp dried shrimp
Woody and earthy rather than briny. It carries a stir-fry base well and falls short in a Thai salad where the shrimp is the point.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: Fresh shrimp in place of dried. Fresh is about 75 percent water and mild; one tablespoon of dried carries the savour of a handful of fresh ones, so the sauce comes out watery and flat.

The shells are the free half of the ingredient. Simmered 15 minutes with a smashed garlic clove they make a stock better than anything on this page.
