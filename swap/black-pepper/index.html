What to use instead of black pepper

It depends entirely on what it's doing in the dish. Here's the job, what else does it, and what to skip.

Advertisement

The pungent heat a dish is built around

Piperine is fat-soluble, which is why pepper blooms in butter or toasts in a dry pan before the pasta water goes in. In cacio e pepe it is a main ingredient.

White peppercorns, ground freshRan out
1:1
Heat lands further back in the throat and brings a faint fermented smell. No black flecks, so a pale sauce stays pale.

Grains of paradise, crackedRan out
1:1
Same order of heat with a cardamom and ginger edge behind it.
Where to buy it →
Sichuan peppercorn + white pepperRan out
1 tsp Sichuan + 1/4 tsp white per 1 tsp black
Numbing and citrusy. You end up with a different dish, which is worth doing on purpose but will surprise anyone expecting cacio e pepe.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: Pre-ground pepper from a tub for cacio e pepe. The aroma oils have been gone for months, so you get grey dust and mild bitterness where the dish needs an actual sting.

Background seasoning in a rub, gravy or braise

It props up salt and adds a low bitter edge that stops rich food tasting flat, all at quantities small enough not to register as heat.

White pepperRan out
3/4 tsp per 1 tsp black
Same seasoning job without visible specks. Slightly barnyard smell that disappears in a cooked sauce.
Where to buy it →
CayenneRan out
1/4 tsp per 1 tsp black pepper
Sharper and further forward on the tongue, with none of the woody note. Easy to overshoot.
Where to buy it →
Pink peppercorns, crushedRan out
1:1
Fruity and mild with almost no heat, so the dish tastes under-seasoned unless you add a pinch more salt.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: Chilli flakes measured one for one. They are several times hotter, the burn sits at the front of the mouth, and they float on a gravy in red specks instead of dissolving through it.
