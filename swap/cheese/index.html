What to use instead of cheese

It depends entirely on what it's doing in the dish. Here's the job, what else does it, and what to skip.

Advertisement

Cheese that melts into a smooth sheet and pulls

Young cheese still holds enough moisture for the protein to flow when it heats, so it goes liquid and stretches rather than separating.

Low-moisture mozzarellaRan out
1:1 by weight
The best pull of anything here and very mild. It browns less than cheddar, so a grilled cheese looks paler.
Where to buy it →
Monterey Jack or OaxacaRan out
1:1 by weight
Melts smooth with almost no oiling out. Mild enough that a quesadilla needs salt or salsa alongside.
Where to buy it →
Gruyere or racletteRan out
1:1 by weight
Nutty and much more assertive. Higher fat, so it can weep oil if the pan is too hot, and it browns fast.
Where to buy it →
Plant-based cheese shredsDairy-free
1:1 by volume, covered with a lid for the last 2 min
Only melts properly under trapped steam, and it sets stiff again within about a minute off the heat. Serve immediately.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: Aged cheddar or Parmesan on a grilled cheese. Long ageing drives out the moisture that lets the protein flow and concentrates the calcium bonds, so instead of melting the cheese splits into a pool of oil and a rubbery clot.

Salty seasoning grated over a finished plate

Here it is not supposed to melt. A dry, hard cheese seasons each mouthful the way salt would, with a bit of texture.

CotijaRan out
1:1 by volume
Drier and saltier and it holds its crumbles on a hot tostada. Tastes considerably sharper than shredded cheddar.
Where to buy it →
FetaRan out
1:1 by volume
Wetter and tangier. It softens on anything hot and reads Greek rather than Mexican.
Where to buy it →
Parmesan or pecorino, microplanedRan out
3/4 the volume
Much saltier per spoonful, so use less. It melts into a savoury film rather than sitting in crumbles.
Where to buy it →
Nutritional yeastDairy-free
2 tbsp per 1/4 cup grated cheese, plus a pinch of salt
Savoury and cheesy-adjacent with no salt of its own and no texture. It goes damp and clumps if the plate is wet.
What doesn't work: Pre-shredded bagged cheese as the finishing sprinkle. It is dusted with potato starch and cellulose to stop it caking, which mutes the flavour and leaves a chalky coating on the tongue that you notice most when the cheese is not melted.

Cheese that binds a baked filling together

Melted protein and fat set as they cool and hold everything around them, so a stuffed pepper or a casserole cuts into portions rather than collapsing.

Low-moisture mozzarellaRan out
1:1 by weight
Binds tightest of the common cheeses. Mild, so the filling itself has to carry the flavour.
Where to buy it →
Provolone or young goudaRan out
1:1 by weight
Similar bind with considerably more flavour. Slightly more oil comes out at the edges of the dish.
Where to buy it →
Cream cheese, cubed through the fillingRan out
4 oz per 8 oz shredded cheese
Binds harder and stays creamy rather than stringy. It makes the filling richer and heavier.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: Fresh mozzarella in a baked filling. It is more than half water, so instead of binding it releases a puddle into the dish, the peppers sit in liquid and the filling slides out when you serve it.
