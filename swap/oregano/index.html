What to use instead of dried oregano

It depends entirely on what it's doing in the dish. Here's the job, what else does it, and what to skip.

Advertisement

Pungent dry herb that survives a long cook or high heat

Carvacrol is stable and fat-soluble, so unlike most herbs oregano holds up through an hour in a sauce or under a grill. Drying concentrates it, which is why dried is the standard form here rather than a compromise.

What doesn't work: Mexican oregano where the recipe means Greek. It is a different plant in the verbena family, citrusy and resinous rather than peppery, and in a horiatiki or a souvlaki marinade it fights the feta and the lemon instead of backing them up.

Rubbed finishing herb on grilled meat or a raw salad

Crushed between the fingers as it goes on, dried oregano releases its oil directly onto the surface where you taste it undiluted. This is the one job where dried genuinely beats fresh.

Fresh oregano, chopped, added after restingRan out
1 tbsp fresh per 1 tsp dried
Greener and much milder, and it wilts the instant it touches hot meat, so it goes on at the end rather than before the grill.
Where to buy it →
What doesn't work: Fresh oregano measured spoon for spoon against dried. Fresh is about three quarters water, so a teaspoon of it carries roughly a third of the flavour; a Greek salad seasoned that way tastes of olive oil and nothing else.
