One ships anywhere; three are regional, and we'd rather say so than pretend the category is national.

Straight Answers
Halal Meal Prep & Chef-Made Meals FAQ
What does 'halal' usually mean on a meal-prep menu?

Most often it means the kitchen buys meat labeled halal from a supplier and stops there. It says nothing about sauces, marinades, cooking wine, or whether the same pans handle non-halal food. A certifier's mark means an outside body audited the sourcing and the kitchen. If a service can't name one, you're taking its word for the whole chain.

Why are most of these regional instead of nationwide?

Fresh, never-frozen meals have a few days of life and move by van, not freight. The options that cross the country are frozen or shelf-stable, which is a different product. If you want food cooked this week, it has to come from a kitchen near you — so the certified local ones are worth knowing by name.

How long do prepped meals keep?

Fresh ones generally hold four to five days refrigerated from the cook date, which is why services deliver weekly and why seven meals is the standard pack. You can freeze the back half on arrival, though rice and cream sauces come back rougher than braises and stews. Frozen trays keep for months.

Is meal prep cheaper than cooking?

No, and it isn't trying to be. At roughly $9 to $13 a meal you're paying a kitchen to shop, cook, and wash up. It competes with takeout, not with groceries. Where it earns its place is portion control and the fact that certified halal takeout is thin on the ground in most cities.
