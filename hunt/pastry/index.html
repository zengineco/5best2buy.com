Straight Answers
Croissants & Pastry FAQ
Why is good pastry so hard to buy shipped?

Baked croissants go stale within a day — the crisp, shattering exterior collapses fast — so shipping them baked doesn't work. The fix that's emerged is shipping them raw and frozen so you bake them fresh at home. Outside of that, a great croissant is still mostly a go-to-a-good-bakery thing.

How do you bake frozen croissants at home?

Most need a proof (leaving them out a few hours to rise) and then a hot oven for 15–20 minutes — follow the maker's card. The reward is a croissant fresh from your own oven, which beats anything you can buy pre-baked. Some products (like Wildgrain's) skip the proof and go straight from freezer to oven.

What makes real laminated pastry different?

Lamination is folding a block of butter into dough over and over to create dozens of paper-thin alternating layers. In the oven the butter steams and pushes those layers apart, giving the flaky, hollow crumb. Cheap 'croissants' fake it with shortcuts and margarine and come out dense and greasy.

Is this shelf smaller than the others on purpose?

Yes — honestly, independent laminated-pastry that ships DTC is a thin field; most great croissants are local. We list the real bake-at-home makers rather than pad the shelf with wholesale suppliers or reheated commodity pastry. If you make it and ship it, the open spot is yours.
