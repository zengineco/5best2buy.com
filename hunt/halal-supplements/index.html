Halal Vitamins & Supplements Worth the Hunt

The supplement aisle is the single worst-labeled corner of a halal kitchen. Most capsule shells are gelatin, usually porcine; gummy vitamins are gelatin too; glycerin, magnesium stearate, and liquid herbal extracts can all trace back to animal fat or ethanol. None of that shows up as anything but 'other ingredients.' These three brands are certified by name — IFANCA in one case, Islamic Services of America in the other two — and each publishes it.

Straight Answers
Halal Vitamins & Supplements FAQ
What's actually non-halal in a vitamin?

Usually the capsule shell — standard gelatin capsules are made from pork or non-zabiha bovine hide and bone. After that: gelatin in gummies, glycerin that can be animal-derived, magnesium stearate from tallow, ethanol used in liquid herbal extracts, and carmine (crushed insects) as a red colorant. None of these have to disclose their source on a US label.

Is a 'vegetarian capsule' the same as halal certified?

It solves the biggest problem and leaves the rest. A veggie cap is usually HPMC (plant cellulose), so the shell is fine, but it says nothing about glycerin, stearates, alcohol-extracted botanicals, or the equipment the plant also runs. Certification covers the whole formula and the facility; 'veggie capsule' covers one component.

Can gummy vitamins ever be halal?

Yes, if they're set with pectin instead of gelatin, which several halal-certified brands now do. The texture is slightly different — a little firmer and less springy than a gelatin gummy — and pectin versions are often vegan-labeled too. If a gummy doesn't say pectin, assume it's gelatin.

Is fish oil automatically halal?

The oil itself generally is, since fish don't require ritual slaughter under most rulings. The capsule is the question: many fish oils use bovine or porcine gelatin softgels. Fish gelatin and plant-based softgels both exist, but you'll only know which one you're holding if the brand is certified or spells it out.
