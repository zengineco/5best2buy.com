Straight Answers
Muffins & Quick Breads FAQ
What actually counts as a quick bread?

A quick bread is leavened with baking powder or baking soda instead of yeast, so there's no rising and waiting — you mix and bake. Banana bread, zucchini bread, muffins, scones, and most coffee cakes are quick breads. That fast chemical leavening is what gives them a tender, cakey crumb rather than the chew of a yeast loaf.

Why does bakery coffee cake taste so different from the boxed kind?

Real coffee cake gets its richness from sour cream or butter and its texture from a proper streusel or cinnamon-nut swirl folded through the batter. Boxed and grocery versions cut those costs with oil, less egg, and preservatives for shelf life, which flattens the flavor and the crumb. The difference is mostly fat quality and freshness.

How long do these keep, and can I freeze them?

Because they're preservative-free, most keep a few days well-wrapped at room temperature, longer in the fridge. They freeze beautifully — wrap tightly and freeze up to a couple of months, then thaw at room temperature. Coffee cakes and nut breads actually reheat nicely; a few seconds of warmth wakes the butter and cinnamon back up.

Are these good for gifting?

Very — coffee cakes and quick breads are among the most giftable bakes because they travel well, don't need refrigeration for a day or two, and several of these makers pack them in tins. They read as more thoughtful than a generic gift basket and they're actually eaten, not regifted. Just note any nut allergies before you send.
