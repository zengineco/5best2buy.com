Halal Beef Bacon & Breakfast Meats Worth the Hunt

Bacon is the thing people who keep halal say they miss most, and the beef version is harder to make than it sounds. You're curing and smoking beef belly, a cut with far less fat than pork belly, and plenty of what's sold as beef bacon is just thin-sliced brisket with smoke flavor. These four cure it properly, and every one of them names the body that audits it.

Straight Answers
Halal Beef Bacon & Breakfast Meats FAQ
What is beef bacon actually made from?

Properly, from beef belly — the navel plate, the same anatomical cut as pork belly. It's cured and smoked the same way. A lot of cheaper 'beef bacon' is sliced brisket or round instead, which is leaner and eats more like dried beef than bacon. If the maker doesn't say belly, it's probably not.

Does it taste like pork bacon?

No, and it's better to expect that going in. It's beefier and less sweet, there's much less fat to render, and it goes from chewy to leathery quickly. Treat it as its own thing — good beef bacon in a sandwich or crumbled over eggs is excellent, it just isn't a swap.

How do I cook beef bacon without ruining it?

Start it in a cold pan over medium-low and go slower than you would with pork. There's little fat to render, so it can scorch before it crisps, and you may want a touch of oil to start. In an oven, 375°F on a rack for 10 to 15 minutes gives you more even control. Pull it while it still has some give.

Why does halal beef bacon cost so much?

Beef belly is a small cut with competing demand — it also goes to navel pastrami — and each animal yields very little of it. Add a hand-slaughter run, a certification audit, a multi-day cure and smoke, and cold shipping, and you land where these prices are. It's a special-occasion breakfast, not a Tuesday one.
